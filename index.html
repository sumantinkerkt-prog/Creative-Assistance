How it works

1. Group your work into projects

A project is simply a container for related work — a client job, a video, a launch, a piece of writing. Give it a name, a colour and a one-line description so it's easy to spot later.

2. Move tasks through stages

Every task starts as an Idea, becomes In Progress, waits in Review, then lands in Done. Seeing the pile shift from left to right is what makes progress feel real.

3. Add dates only where they matter

Due dates are optional on purpose. Add one and the task starts warning you as the day approaches, then flags itself as overdue. Leave it blank and it stays quiet.

4. Keep your small tools nearby

Quick notes, a word counter, a focus timer, colour palettes and a checklist all live on one page — so you stop hunting for five different websites mid-task.

Why it works without an account

Most planning tools ask you to sign up, then keep your work on someone else's computer. This one doesn't. Your projects, tasks and notes are written straight into your own browser's storage, on the device you're using right now.

That trade-off buys you three things:

  • Privacy by default. There is no account, no tracking of your content, and no server holding your work. It cannot be read by anyone else because it never leaves your machine.
  • It's genuinely instant. Nothing waits on a network. Every change is saved the moment you make it.
  • It costs nothing to run. No servers means no subscription, now or later.

The honest downside: your work lives on this device in this browser. It won't appear on your phone, and clearing your browsing data will erase it. That's exactly why the Save a backup button above exists — it hands you a single file holding everything, which you can restore here at any time, or on any other device.
